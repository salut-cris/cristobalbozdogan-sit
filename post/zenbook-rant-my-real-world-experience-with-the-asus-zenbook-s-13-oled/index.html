Zenbook Rant : My Real-World Experience with the ASUS Zenbook S 13 OLED

Cristobal Bozdogan · Oct 19, 2026

I bought the ASUS Zenbook S 13 OLED (UX5304VA) expecting a sleek, ultraportable powerhouse for getting work done on the move. It’s impressively thin and lightweight, but actually living with this machine day-to-day has been a lesson in trade-offs.

Here is what it’s really like once you strip away the marketing promises.

The Coffee Shop Embarrassment Factor

The biggest issue with forcing high-end hardware into an ultra-thin chassis is thermal management. Under pretty basic multitasking—browsing with a bunch of tabs, working in spreadsheets, or managing documents—the tiny internal fans spin up fast.

Because they're small, they don't just push air; they emit a high-pitched fan whine. Sitting in a quiet coffee shop or library, pulling out a premium laptop that sounds like a miniature jet taking off is plain embarrassing. You shouldn't have to tweak power plans just to stop your machine from drawing glares from the next table.

Thermal Hotspots Where Your Hands Go

It’s not just noisy—it gets hot right where you need to touch it. Heat dissipates right into the middle of the keyboard. After sustained typing sessions, the central keys get uncomfortably warm to use, which completely ruins the typing experience if you're trying to power through an afternoon of work without an external keyboard.

The OLED Screen: Great Picture, Unpredictable Flaws

The 2.8K OLED display itself looks fantastic—deep blacks, vivid colors, and great contrast. But it comes with two massive real-world headaches:

  • Random Flickering: The display will randomly flicker for no apparent reason. It’s an annoying distraction when you’re trying to focus.
  • Mirror-Like Reflections: The panel is highly reflective. Under bright office lights or near a coffee shop window, you're constantly staring back at your own reflection unless you max out the brightness (which tanks the battery life even faster).

Living the Dongle Life & Bloatware Out of the Box

  • Ports (or Lack Thereof): Being this thin means sacrificing built-in connectivity. Carrying a USB-C hub or dongle everywhere is mandatory if you want to plug in an external monitor, an SD card, or standard USB devices.
  • Bloatware: Right out of the box, the system was loaded with pre-installed bloatware, pop-ups, and background utility services. Having to spend time cleaning out junk software on a expensive machine just to get clean background performance is unacceptable.

The One Thing They Got Right

To give credit where it's due: the trackpad is incredible. The glass surface is smooth, accurate, spacious, and palm rejection works flawlessly. It’s easily the best input component on the laptop.

Verdict

The Zenbook S 13 OLED is a nice piece of industrial design on paper, but in practice, extreme thinness compromises thermals, noise, display stability, and basic usability. If you need a laptop for actual sustained work in public spaces, prepare to deal with loud fans, warm keys, reflective glass, and a bag full of dongles.

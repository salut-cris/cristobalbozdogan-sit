What MNF26 Taught Me About Bringing People Together

Cristobal Bozdogan · Oct 22, 2026

A few weeks have passed since the conclusion of the Micronutrient Forum 2026 (MNF26) in Accra, Ghana, and I finally have some time to reflect on the experience.

When you work in sponsorship and industry relations, people often see the final product: the packed exhibition hall, the sponsored sessions, the networking conversations, the meetings taking place over coffee, and the photos that appear on LinkedIn once the event is over.

What they don't see are the hundreds of conversations, emails, calls, revisions, and problem-solving moments that take place in the months leading up to the event.

For me, MNF26 was a reminder that successful congresses are ultimately about people.

Over the past year, I had the opportunity to work with organisations from across the global nutrition and health ecosystem, including NGOs, foundations, academic institutions, private sector partners, and international organisations. Each came to the event with different objectives, priorities, and expectations. My role was to help ensure that those goals could be translated into meaningful participation at the congress while maintaining a positive experience for everyone involved.

One of the most rewarding aspects of the process was watching partnerships develop over time. Conversations that started as exploratory calls months before the event eventually became exhibition booths, sponsored sessions, networking opportunities, and collaborations that brought valuable content and perspectives to the scientific community.

Walking through the exhibition hall in Accra was particularly satisfying. Seeing an idea move from an initial enquiry to a signed agreement and finally to a physical presence on the show floor never gets old. Every booth represented months of planning, coordination, and teamwork between sponsors, organisers, suppliers, and colleagues working behind the scenes.

What made MNF26 especially memorable was the quality and diversity of the people attending. Researchers, policymakers, programme implementers, donors, healthcare professionals, industry representatives, and advocates were all gathered in one place, united by a common goal: improving nutrition outcomes around the world.

In a period where virtual communication dominates much of our professional lives, there is still tremendous value in bringing people together face-to-face. Some of the most productive conversations happen outside the meeting rooms: in the exhibition hall, during coffee breaks, while walking between sessions, or during an impromptu introduction between colleagues.

Those moments create opportunities that simply cannot be scheduled.

Professionally, MNF26 was one of the most challenging and rewarding projects I have worked on to date. Large international congresses require constant balancing of priorities, adapting to changing circumstances, and finding solutions that work for multiple stakeholders. No event is perfect, and there are always lessons to learn, but that's also what makes this industry so engaging.

The event also reinforced something I have come to appreciate throughout my career: successful congresses are never the result of one individual. They are built by teams.

I was fortunate to work alongside outstanding colleagues who consistently went above and beyond, particularly during the final weeks leading up to the congress. The willingness of people to step in, solve problems, and support one another is often what determines whether an event simply functions or truly succeeds.

Looking back, I am proud of the role I played in helping deliver an event that brought together a global community committed to tackling some of the world's most important nutrition challenges.

I am equally grateful for the trust placed in me by sponsors, partners, clients, and colleagues throughout the process.

As events professionals, we often move immediately to the next project. The inbox fills up again, new deadlines appear, and attention shifts forward. Before doing that, however, it is worth pausing to appreciate what was accomplished.

MNF26 was a valuable reminder of why I enjoy this work so much. It is not just about contracts, floorplans, sponsorship packages, or logistics. It is about creating spaces where meaningful conversations can happen, relationships can grow, and ideas can move forward.

And when thousands of people gather around a shared purpose, the impact extends far beyond the event itself.

Thank you to everyone who contributed to making MNF26 such a memorable experience. I am excited to see where the conversations started in Accra lead next.
